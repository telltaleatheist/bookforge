You are reading one block of a book that is about to be narrated by a text-to-speech voice. What is left is what only a reader of the sentence can settle.

THE CLASSES YOU ARE BEING ASKED ABOUT

1. NUMBER RESIDUE — anything the rules above declined: a bare four-digit number that is a year or a quantity depending on the sentence, an abbreviated range, a bare decimal, a heading number. Read them exactly as the rules above say.

2. ABBREVIATIONS a narrator says in full: "Dr." is "Doctor", "St." is "Saint" or "Street" depending on the sentence, "Mt." is "Mount", "e.g." is "for example", "i.e." is "that is", "etc." is "et cetera", "vs." is "versus", "&" is "and". Leave "Mr.", "Mrs.", "Ms." exactly as printed — every voice already says those correctly, and expanding them adds nothing.

3. ALL-CAPS RUNS. An acronym said as letters is spelled out with spaces — "FBI" is "F B I", "NSDAP" is "N S D A P" — because a voice handed "FBI" may try to say it as a word. An acronym said AS a word is left alone: NASA, NATO, UNESCO, laser, radar. A word in capitals for EMPHASIS is written in ordinary case — "he SAID so" is "he said so" — because the capitals are typography, not sound. Write the letters in the case they were printed in ("F B I", never "f b i") and the emphasis reading in ordinary lower case.

4. BRACKETED INSERTIONS. Two different edits, and which one depends on what is inside.
   APPARATUS is not spoken and is REMOVED whole, brackets and all, replaced by nothing but the surrounding spacing: "[sic]", "[12]", "[ed.]", "[...]", "(sic)", "(emphasis added)", "(see page twelve)", "(Kershaw 1993)", "(12)".
   AN INTERPOLATION OF WORDS in SQUARE brackets is READ, not removed: the edit drops the brackets and keeps every word — "[he said]" becomes "he said". Never delete words.
   A PARENTHESIS is the author’s own punctuation and stays exactly as printed unless its contents are one of the apparatus shapes above. "(he was lying)" and "(note she wept)" are the book. If you are not certain, leave it.

5. A SPACED HYPHEN used as a dash — "the man - who had waited - left" — is an em dash in disguise. Replace the spaced hyphen with an em dash. A hyphen inside a compound ("far-right") and a hyphen between numbers are NOT this and must be left.

6. ROMAN NUMERALS are read as words ONLY where a book prints a numeral: after a part word ("Part IV" is "Part Four", "Chapter IX", "Book II"), after a name ("Henry VIII" is "Henry the Eighth", "Pius XII"), or before a century ("the XIX century"). Anywhere else a run of capitals is an ACRONYM even when its letters are all I V X L C D M: "MIX", "MD", "CD", "MM", "XL", "IX" and "CIV" are read as their own letters spaced, or left alone. A roman numeral that is a citation or a volume — "Document II 9/34", "vol. iii" — is apparatus and stays exactly as printed.

7. DIGIT RESIDUE. If a digit is still printed anywhere in the block after your edits, you have missed one. Go back and read it, or leave it deliberately because it is a code.

FOOTNOTE AND REFERENCE MARKERS ARE NOT YOURS. A superscript reference number, a dagger, an asterisk used as a reference: the render door removes those from the narration copy itself, deterministically, and an edit that tried to would be refused because it deletes text without saying anything in its place. Leave them exactly where they are.

THE RULES THAT BOUND EVERY EDIT

- KEEP THE PUNCTUATION. Every comma, semicolon, colon, dash, quote and bracket the `find` prints outside the word you are changing must appear again in the `replace`, in the same order. "Dr. Kempner; they" may become "Doctor Kempner; they" and never "Doctor Kempner they". If the abbreviation ends the span and a capital follows it in the block, its period may be ending a sentence — keep it: "Oxford St. The rain" becomes "Oxford Street. The rain".
- ONE TOKEN PER EDIT. The replacement must repeat every word of the `find`, in order, EXCEPT the single token the class is about — the abbreviation, the run of capitals, the roman numeral. "Dr. Kempner" may become "Doctor Kempner"; it may not become "Doctor Kempner of Berlin", and "Kempner" may not become "Kempler". An edit that changes any other word is refused.
- NEVER PARAPHRASE. You may change the SPOKEN FORM of a span. You may not improve a sentence, reorder it, shorten it, translate it, or replace a word with a synonym. If the only change you can think of is a better way of saying it, make no edit.
- Every `find` is verbatim and occurs exactly once in the TARGET. If a span occurs twice, extend the find with the words around it until it is unique, or leave it.
- Keep every edit SHORT. An edit is a span whose reading differs, not a clause.
- Never edit the PREVIOUS or NEXT block. They are there so you can tell a year from a quantity and a Saint from a Street.
- An empty edit list is the right answer for most blocks. Ordinary prose needs nothing.
